One-time setup needed

Chat with Gregorio keeps your messages in a free Firebase project so they sync between your phones. That project isn't connected yet.

  1. Create a free Firebase project (about 5 minutes).
  2. Paste its settings into firebase-config.js.
  3. Publish, then install on both phones.

Add it to your Home Screen first

Tap Share (the square with an arrow — on newer iPhones it's inside the ⋯ button) → Add to Home Screen → Add. If it shows an Open as Web App switch, leave it on. Then open the chat from the new icon and enter your name there.

Opened this from another app (WhatsApp, Messages, Mail…)? Use its share or ⋯ button → Open in Safari first. On an iPhone, a Safari tab and the Home Screen icon count as two different phones, and the chat only has room for two — setting up here would use up one of the places. Just want to see the demo?

Just set this up and your partner hasn't joined yet? Then the other place is probably held by your computer, an earlier try, or (on an iPhone) a Safari tab instead of the Home Screen icon. Open ⚙ Settings on the device that is in the chat and tap Disconnect next to the stray entry.

Import old messages

Bring your conversation from WhatsApp or your phone's text messages into this chat. Old messages are translated when you tap them, so they don't use up the free daily limit.

How do I get the file?

WhatsApp on iPhone: open the chat → tap the name at the top → Export Chat → Without Media → Save to Files. Then choose that file here (a .zip called "WhatsApp Chat - …").

WhatsApp on Android: open the chat → ⋮ → More → Export chat → Without media → choose Drive (or email it to yourself). Android keeps no copy of its own — pick a place to save it. Then choose that .txt file here.

Android text messages: install the free SMS Backup & Restore app → make a backup of messages, saved to the phone. Choose its .xml file here — every conversation is in it, and you'll pick which one on the next step. Imported from your partner's phone? Then the row "texts sent from the phone that made the backup" is them, not you.

iPhone text messages: Apple doesn't let apps read them. If your partner is on Android, back up from their phone instead — it holds both sides of the conversation.
